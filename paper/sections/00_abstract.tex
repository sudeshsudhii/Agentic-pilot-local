\begin{abstract}
Browser agents can report unfinished tasks as done, and many send every observation to a hosted model. Agentic Pilot is an open-source browser agent with local models that may report completion only when a rule-based predicate over the observed page holds, storing each decision's inputs as evidence. We formalize this evidence-gated completion with false-completion and false-rejection rates and evaluate it at component level, in the agent's real verification node with stubbed I/O. On 42 held-out constructed states frozen before testing, the gate withheld 9 of 22 unsatisfied states (95\% interval 0.23--0.61) and rejected 1 of 20 satisfied ones. It was correct on more states than a dispatch-only rule (exact McNemar $p=0.021$) but not significantly more than an error-check-only detector ($p=0.070$), and below the 20 of 23 withheld on its design states, because its checks are keyed to request wording. A decision costs a median of 27\,ms and no model call. Privacy holds in a narrow sense: by default the model client refuses non-loopback endpoints, and in 70 episodes with a scripted stand-in model server the agent's Python processes connected only to 127.0.0.1. Measurements also show that memory retrieval lacks a relevance threshold and recovery misses absent confirmations. A seeded simulation, revised post hoc, shows that the gate's benefit depends on the unmeasured rate of premature completion claims; its outputs are not measurements of the agent. The evaluation covers component-level validation and simulation-based analysis under controlled conditions; an evaluation harness for task-level studies is released.
\end{abstract}
