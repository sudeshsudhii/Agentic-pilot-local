\begin{abstract}
Browser agents can report a task as finished when it is not, and many send every observation to a hosted model. Agentic Pilot is an open-source browser agent with local models that may report completion only when a rule-based predicate over the observed page holds, storing the inputs of each decision as evidence. We formalize this evidence-gated completion and its false completion and false rejection rates. \emph{Measured at component level}, inside the agent's live verification node, the gate withheld 20 of 23 unsatisfied design states (95\% interval 0.68--0.95) and rejected 1 of 15 satisfied ones; on 42 held-out states frozen before testing it withheld 9 of 22 (0.23--0.61; exact McNemar $p=0.004$ against a dispatch-only rule), because its answer check depends on request wording. A decision costs a median of 27\,ms and no model call. The model client refuses non-loopback endpoints, and in 70 episodes with a scripted stand-in for the model server the agent's Python processes connected only to loopback addresses. Memory retrieval has no relevance threshold, and recovery does not recognize a missing confirmation. \emph{In a seeded simulation} built on these measurements and stated assumptions, the gate reduced episode-level false completions (19 against 28 of 100 synthetic scenarios) without improving decision accuracy (45 against 43 of 71 claims); these outcomes are conditional on the assumptions. \emph{No end-to-end evaluation with a language model was executed}; its harness is released.
\end{abstract}
