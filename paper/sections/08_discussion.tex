\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports} On constructed states the gate withholds 41\% of unsatisfied held-out states and is correct on significantly more held-out states than a dispatch-only rule, at tens of milliseconds and no model call; its coverage of new phrasings, not its cost, is its binding limit, and some of its defects appeared only inside the live verification node. The evidence does not show that the agent completes tasks. The simulation (Section~\ref{sec:sim-results}) adds no empirical support; it shows that under the stated assumptions the benefit of the gate depends on the unmeasured premature-claim rate and is paid for in false rejections.

\paragraph{Rules and model judges} The held-out results argue against extending hand-written web rules indefinitely: each rule is keyed to the wording it was written for. Model-based judges~\cite{pan2024autonomous,sumyk2025done} cover broader goals but add inference cost and are models judging models~\cite{huang2024cannot}. The self-verification baseline in our harness will measure this alternative on the same tasks. We expect the two to be complementary---exact postconditions where the environment exposes structured state, a judge as a second opinion where it does not---with both inputs kept as evidence.
