\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports} On constructed states the gate withholds 41\% of unsatisfied held-out states and is correct on significantly more held-out states than a dispatch-only rule, though not than its own error check alone ($p=0.070$), at tens of milliseconds and no model call; its coverage of new phrasings, not its cost, is its binding limit, and some of its defects appeared only inside the live verification node. The evidence does not show that the agent completes tasks. The simulation (Section~\ref{sec:sim-results}) adds no empirical support; it shows that under the stated assumptions the benefit of the gate depends on the unmeasured premature-claim rate and is paid for in false rejections.

\paragraph{Rules and model judges} Hand-written rules are keyed to the wording they were written for; model-based judges~\cite{pan2024autonomous,sumyk2025done} cover broader goals but add inference cost and are models judging models~\cite{huang2024cannot}. The two are likely complementary.
