\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Agentic Pilot is a local browser agent built on the rule that an executed action is not a completed task: it may report completion only when a predicate over the observed page holds, and it keeps the inputs of that decision as evidence. The paper offers three kinds of evidence, which should be read separately. \emph{Measured at component level:} in the agent's real verification node, called directly on constructed states with stubbed I/O, the predicate withheld most premature completions on the states it was designed on and 41\% on states frozen before testing, at a median of 27\,ms and no model call, so its coverage of new phrasings, not its cost, is the limit; by default the model client connects only to a loopback endpoint, and in 70 episodes with a stand-in model server the agent's Python processes connected only to loopback addresses, which says nothing about where a real model server sends a request. \emph{Simulated, conditional on stated assumptions:} the gate's benefit depends on how often the planner claims completion prematurely and is paid for in false rejections. Component measurements, not the simulation, show that memory retrieval has no relevance threshold and that recovery does not recognize a missing confirmation; the simulated gains of a relevance filter and a confirmation check follow from their assumed rates and are not evidence for them. \emph{Not executed:} an end-to-end evaluation with a language model; the harness for it is released with an oracle validated on scripted trajectories, and its results will decide the questions the simulation can only frame.
