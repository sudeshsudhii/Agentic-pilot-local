\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Agentic Pilot runs a browser agent against local models and reports success only when a check on the observed page passes. Separating observation, planning, execution, verification and recovery into graph nodes made it possible to state that rule formally and test it in isolation, inside the live node and on labelled end states. The tests exposed concrete defects, and fixing them raised the share of withheld premature completions on our design states from at most 15 to 20 of 23; held-out states show that such rule fixes generalize only partially. The same structure extends to files, processes and applications once accessibility trees, typed postconditions and deterministic permission tiers replace the DOM, page checks and model-assigned risk. What remains is to replace hand-written rules with typed step predicates, measure the effect of memory and recovery, and evaluate the full system on real tasks.
